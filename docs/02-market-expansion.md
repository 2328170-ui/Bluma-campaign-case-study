# Market Expansion Strategy — Lithuania → Europe

## Why start in Lithuania

Lithuania is the right **beachhead** (Geoffrey Moore sense): a market small enough to win
decisively, with conditions that transfer to the next markets.

- **Home advantage:** existing brand recognition, references, and installed base.
- **EU regulatory alignment:** Lithuania operates under the same EU Green Deal / Fit-for-55
  framework as the expansion targets, so the value story is portable.
- **Vilnius as a proof city:** a compact capital where a handful of visible installations
  create disproportionate awareness.
- **Lower cost to learn:** cheaper to run pilots and refine the motion before scaling.

## Expansion logic: rings of similarity

We expand outward in rings of decreasing similarity to the home market, so each step reuses
as much of the previous playbook as possible.

```
   Ring 0  Lithuania            (home / proof)
   Ring 1  Baltics              Latvia, Estonia        — near-identical regulation & culture
   Ring 2  Nordic + Poland      Finland, Sweden, PL    — high green-policy maturity, budget
   Ring 3  EU core              Germany, Netherlands,  — large, mandate-driven, ESG capital
                                 France, Austria
```

### Why this order

| Ring | Pull factors | Why now / why later |
|---|---|---|
| Baltics | Same regulatory regime, shared media, low localization cost | First — cheapest replication |
| Nordics + Poland | Mature green-roof policy (e.g. mandates, subsidies), strong public budgets | Second — high willingness-to-pay |
| EU core | Largest TAM, green-roof mandates already exist (e.g. several German/Dutch/French cities) | Last — highest competition, needs proof + scale to enter credibly |

## Market sizing (top-down → bottom-up)

We size by **addressable green surface** rather than just headcount, because the product is
priced per m².

**Top-down anchor (full European-scale scenario in the model):**
- 15 target cities × ~200,000 m² of realistically convertible roof/wall surface = **3,000,000 m²**.
- This is the basis for the €148.76M / €56M headline figures.

**Bottom-up sanity check (per city):**
- A mid-size EU city has millions of m² of flat roof; converting even 1–3% over the program
  horizon yields the ~200,000 m² assumption. Conservative and defensible.

## Three buyer segments, three value stories

Green infrastructure is bought by three buyers who care about different things. The whole GTM
motion is built around serving each correctly.

| Segment | Buyer | Primary value | Lead metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B2G** (public) | City sustainability / public-works depts | Compliance + public savings (stormwater, health, heat) | €148.76M savings story |
| **B2B** (commercial) | Developers, REITs, corporate ESG leads | Asset value, ESG reporting, tenant appeal | Property uplift + CO₂ |
| **Hospitality / SMB** | Café & restaurant owners | Footfall, brand, differentiation | Visibility + branded activation |

## Competitive positioning

BLUMA does not win on being the cheapest panel supplier. It wins on being the **partner that
quantifies and proves public value**:

- **vs. generic landscapers:** we bring an impact model and regulatory framing, not just plants.
- **vs. large construction firms:** we are modular, faster, and marketing-native.
- **vs. doing nothing:** the model shows green infrastructure pays for itself in avoided public costs.

**Positioning statement:**
> For European cities and developers under pressure to hit climate and livability targets,
> BLUMA is the green-infrastructure partner that turns roofs and walls into quantified public
> value — measured in euros saved and CO₂ removed — and proves it with visible, fast installs.
